## Recent Price Action

Novo Nordisk is trading near its **52-week low of $43.08**, down nearly 50% from its 2025 highs at $93.80. The stock crashed ~18% on Feb 4 after management pre-released shockingly weak 2026 guidance (sales and operating profit both declining 5-13%). However, shares staged a **strong 9.92% rebound on Feb 6** after the FDA announced a crackdown on illegal compounded GLP-1 drug copies, which have been eroding Novo's branded sales. After-hours trading pushed the stock to $49.89.

**Q4 2025 Earnings (Feb 4):** Beat on both lines — EPS $1.02 vs $0.92 expected (+10.87%), revenue $12.53B vs $11.99B expected (+4.5%). The beat was overshadowed by the 2026 outlook.

## Catalysts

### Bullish

1. **FDA Compounded GLP-1 Crackdown** — FDA committed to targeting illegal mass-marketed copies of semaglutide; directly protects Novo's revenue base. Triggered the Feb 6 rebound.
2. **Wegovy Pill Launch (approved Dec 2025)** — 170,000+ patients in the first ~4 weeks, more than double any prior branded anti-obesity drug. Starting dose priced at $149. Available at 70,000+ pharmacies.
3. **CagriSema vs Zepbound Data (March 2026)** — High-stakes head-to-head trial results expected by end of March. A win here could be transformative for sentiment.
4. **CagriSema FDA Filing** — Phase 3 REIMAGINE trial showed superiority over Wegovy in diabetes patients (14% vs 10% weight loss). FDA review underway, potential approval late 2026/early 2027.
5. **Medicare Part D Obesity Coverage** — Expected mid-2026, opening a massive new patient population.
6. **Share Buyback** — DKK 3.8B buyback running Feb 4–May 4, 2026, plus DKK 15B annual program providing a floor.
7. **Deep Value Territory** — P/E of ~13x is historically cheap for Novo. Analyst consensus targets imply 17-24% upside.
8. **Short Interest Declining** — Down 13.1% from December to 28.16M shares (0.6% float, 1.3 days to cover). Bears are covering.

### Bearish

1. **2026 Guidance Shock** — Sales and profit declining 5-13% CER; midpoints are 8% below consensus on sales and 10% below on profit.
2. **U.S. Pricing Pressure** — Trump's MFN policy and TrumpRx.gov Medicare/Medicaid pricing deals compress margins.
3. **Eli Lilly Competition** — Zepbound has overtaken Wegovy as the fastest-selling obesity treatment globally.
4. **Patent Expiries** — Semaglutide losing exclusivity in Brazil, Canada, and China.
5. **Gross Margin Erosion** — Down from 84.7% to 81.0% YoY.
6. **Management Turnover** — New CEO (first non-Danish), U.S. chief departing.
7. **Volume-Price Divergence** — Feb 6 rally showed declining volume, a warning the bounce may not sustain without follow-through.
8. **Institutional Net Selling** — 368M shares sold vs 199M bought last quarter.

## Bull vs Bear Case

**Bull:** At 13x P/E with $50B+ in annual revenue, Novo is historically cheap. The Wegovy pill launch is a blockbuster, CagriSema data in March could be a major re-rating catalyst, and the FDA crackdown on compounders directly protects the franchise. Medicare obesity coverage in mid-2026 opens a huge new market. The buyback provides a floor, and short sellers are covering. This is a best-in-class franchise priced for the worst case.

**Bear:** The 2026 guidance is the worst in Novo's modern history — the first year of declining sales and profit. U.S. pricing pressure from the MFN policy is structural, not temporary. Lilly is winning the obesity race with Zepbound. All major moving averages flash Sell. Institutions are net sellers. The Feb 6 bounce showed volume divergence. The CEO himself warned "it will get worse before it gets better." A break below $43 has no clear technical floor.
